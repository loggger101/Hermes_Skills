# Load-bearing model assumptions (economicspace)

From CLAUDE.md "Model assumptions that are load-bearing" + "Correctness invariants". Undoing any of these **silently** corrupts the output — there is no error, just wrong numbers. Read before changing a model term or "fixing" a result that looks wrong.

## Pricing architecture (Stage 2 / mineral_value)

- **Every commodity is priced by destination, not just water.** At an in-space destination a kg is worth its terrestrial price **PLUS** `in_space_utility × launch-cost-avoided`, minus the cost of refining on site. The *plus* is the point: v1.3.0 briefly replaced the terrestrial price instead of adding to it and quietly threw the material itself away (bulk iron $0.50/kg → ~$2,747/kg in LEO). In-space prices are derived through the rocket equation in `delivered_cost_usd_per_kg()`, not tabulated — but the utility factors are engineering judgements, the softest assumption in the pipeline. Treat them as a dial, not a measurement.
- **Utility is per destination and corrections run DOWNWARD.** One table used to serve every in-space destination (olivine captured the same fraction of its freight on Mars' surface as at an empty-space depot). The missing term is local competition: the alternative to importing isn't always launching from Earth. LEO/cislunar keep the base profile (calibration anchor); `IN_SPACE_UTILITY_BY_DESTINATION` discounts the two surfaces against what they can dig up (Mars water 1.00→0.25, carbon 0.40→0.02, silicates 0.25→0.02; Moon water →0.60, iron →0.45). Ni/Co/Cu undiscounted everywhere (no concentrated ore of either body known); carbon undiscounted on the Moon (~100 ppm solar-wind implantation).
- **`mars_orbit` takes the BASE profile and the empty dict is the point.** It sits directly above `mars_surface`'s long override block and reads as an oversight; copying those overrides up into it destroys the destination's meaning. A settlement STANDING ON a crust digs its own water/iron/rock; a depot in 1-sol orbit competes with nothing — everything martian is 3,400 km down a well costing **4,100 m/s of ascent, more than the 3,600 m/s of TMI that brought cargo from Earth**. The ISRU discount carrying the mars_surface result is a property of being ON Mars. It's also the same heliocentric transfer as mars_surface stopped one leg early: capture into a **1-sol elliptical staging orbit (250 × 33,793 km)** costs only **0.90 km/s** vs 2.10 to circularise — binding an ellipse deep at periapsis where Oberth pays; the downleg home is $30,150/kg from orbit vs $96,394 from the surface, so PGMs with no in-space market are worth something again there (zero at a Mars base).
- **`geo`'s overrides run downward on METALS and ROCK — demand-side, not supply-side.** It's the first destination whose discounts mean "nobody launches a kilogram of copper to geostationary orbit: no factory 36,000 km up" rather than "a settlement digs its own ore". Ni/Co/Cu discounted here where the two surfaces leave them alone (no local FACTORY vs no local ORE). Priced $12,526.34/kg; demand 40,000 kg/yr anchored on hardware that exists (~550 GEO satellites × ~70 kg/yr station-keeping) — the smallest in-space market and the saturation control.
- Two things not to "fix": every override runs downward (raising a utility is how this table becomes a way to manufacture viability); prices still RISE with distance — Mars freight is 10.6 kg-in-LEO per kg delivered and dominates, they just no longer rise as fast; volatiles that carried the Mars result rise least (water at Mars now 2.7× its LEO price vs 11× before).
- **A settlement catalyst market for PGMs was considered and rejected** — reason generalises: this module prices each commodity with one $/kg and one market depth, and `in_space_price_usd_per_kg` routes on unit price alone. Gold at a lunar base would route "used in space" at $76,060/kg into a 25 kg/yr catalyst market, beating $30,061/kg into a 3,000,000 kg/yr terrestrial one — a five-order-of-magnitude cliff the router cannot see. Real behaviour is a blend (sell first few kg in space, fly the rest home); needs quantity-aware route choice, not a bigger table.
- **The import budget is split per commodity** (`_DEMAND_SHARE_BY_CLASS`: propellant 0.55 / structural 0.25 / shielding 0.15 / chemical 0.05, asserted to sum to 1.0). Shares are per CLASS because within a class commodities substitute for each other (shielding mass doesn't care whether it arrives as olivine or pyroxene). `annual_market_kg` is now ROUTED: the market that saturates is the one you actually sell into, so a commodity flown home is bounded by terrestrial annual production rather than a depot's import budget (platinum at LEO was capped at the depot's 500 t/yr against the world's real 180 t/yr). This is the one part of v1.7.0 that runs upward — a correction, not a concession.
- **A commodity with no in-space market is NOT worth zero at a depot.** It's worth its terrestrial price MINUS the downleg (`downleg_cost_usd_per_kg`: capsule + TPS + recovery + departure burn, ~$25,400/kg from LEO). Platinum at a depot ≈ $31,300/kg, not $0. `value_route` records which fate each commodity took.
- **The two surface prices are marginal-transport LOWER BOUNDS**: no NRE, no programme overhead, no cadence limit, on a reusable Falcon 9 LEO price. Real CLPS lunar delivery ≈ $1M/kg today at ~100 kg scale vs this model's $21,210/kg. The whole Mars result rests on that framing ("what could this cost at industrial scale").

## Δv / architecture (Stage 3/4)

- **Cislunar is cheaper to reach than LEO and worth more** — the one result that reliably reads as a bug. Capturing into LEO kills the entire arrival hyperbola (~3.6 km/s at v_inf = 3); capturing into an NRHO depot only has to *bind* the orbit, taking the Oberth benefit at low perigee (~0.94 km/s). See `_cislunar_capture_dv_km_s`. Do not "fix" it.
- **GEO capture is a SEARCH because neither route always wins**: direct capture vs Oberth-at-perigee-then-circularise cross over at v_inf = 3.585 km/s (direct cheaper below, Oberth above) — GEO must be *circularised* into (~4× the cislunar binding burn), so the Oberth benefit has less to work with. Cislunar remains cheapest from an asteroid: 0.96 vs 2.05 km/s at best. **GEO's aerocapture is not an aerobrake trim**: drag can only lower the apogee; circularising there is still a flat 1.737 km/s of real burn — copying `DV_AEROBRAKE_TRIM_KM_S` would understate a GEO arrival by 17×.
- **GEO carries two plane changes that must NOT be reconciled**: the arriving kilogram pays 23.44° (ecliptic→equator, 1,730 m/s) while Module 2 prices the launched kilogram's 28.5° Canaveral parking-orbit change (1,836 m/s). Making them agree would put a launch site's latitude into an interplanetary trajectory. Both combine by law of cosines, not addition — a burn that changes speed and direction at once costs less than doing both separately.
- **Mars is a separate heliocentric transfer**, not a scaled Earth return: `_asteroid_to_mars_dv_km_s` terminates the ellipse at 1.524 AU and captures into Mars' well. Approximating off the Earth legs erases the point — a main-belt body is cheaper to deliver to Mars (3.84 km/s) than to Earth (4.13). Mars has an atmosphere so aerocapture is AVAILABLE; `lunar_surface` ignores `use_aerocapture_return` exactly as cislunar does. Since v1.10.0 "available" is literal: where an atmosphere exists the model prices BOTH aerocaptured and propulsive return per asteroid and flies whichever pays — on a slow-arriving target the Δv saved is small and the heat shield isn't worth hauling out.
- **Surface delivery costs are chained per stage, not lumped.** Module 2's `_DELIVERY_LEGS` walks real stages backwards from the payload. Collapsing the lunar chain into one 5,920 m/s burn overstates the Moon by ~2× (10.96 vs 4.99 kg-in-LEO per kg landed) because it throws away staging. Mars' `edl` leg carries a measured surviving-mass fraction (MSL 27.6%, Perseverance 29.8%), not a Δv.
- **Which apsis the transfer meets the target at is a SEARCH, not a rule.** The old estimator used `r_target = aphelion if aphelion >= 1 AU else perihelion` — right for most main-belt bodies, wrong for high-eccentricity ones (aphelion rendezvous = slow transfer + cheap match burn + expensive departure; perihelion the reverse). For a=0.6 e=0.8 the rule cost **18.5 km/s outbound where perihelion needs 12.1**. Both apsides are priced now and resolved against the DESTINATION (a body best met at aphelion for an Earth return can be best met at perihelion for Mars). Bennu, Eros, Itokawa + both reference cases still resolve to aphelion — published validation figures unaffected.
- **How hard to concentrate is searched, not derived.** Grade saturates at `saturation_ratio` = 1/(frac_best × recovery); costs keep climbing; the optimum is usually strictly interior and `evaluate_combo` sweeps for it. Two mistakes already made that LOOKED principled: driving ratio to max (cislunar missions 4× worse), driving it to saturation on principle (~12% worse). The search also always evaluates NOT concentrating at all (`beneficiate=False`) — not the same as ratio 1.0, which would still pay separation recovery loss + array mass for no grade gain. Without that baseline beneficiation can't be declined and stops being weakly dominant. Makes the beneficiated path several times slower; `concentration_search_steps` is the dial.
- **The payload mix is optimised, not specified**: `optimal_payload_mix` is a fractional knapsack over `asteroid_phase_table`, fill with best phase then next. Greedy by $/kg provably optimal (phases divisible, priced per kg). Both bounds (content and purity) fall out of it — do NOT reintroduce them as separate clamps.
- **The mission architecture is searched too** (v1.10.0): vehicle × propellant × return mode × propellant sourcing × rendezvous apsis × concentration ratio, resolved per body. Aerocapture/ISRU used to be catalog-wide switches — a target whose best mission was propulsive got flown aerocaptured because some other asteroid wanted it. **If you add another architecture choice, add it to this search rather than `CalcConfig` as a global**, and check the never-worse invariant afterwards.
- **Δv must stay per-asteroid** (`use_per_asteroid_dv`). Before v1.4.0 every asteroid got the same Δv → max_payload_kg, total_cost_usd, mission_duration_yr, vehicle, propellant single-valued across an entire catalog; ranking was composition-only and accessibility had no effect. **If you ever see those columns collapse to one unique value again**, that flag is off or orbital elements aren't reaching Stage 4.
- **Electric propulsion needs its Δv penalty** (`dv_penalty_factor`): the rocket equation ignores thrust, trajectories don't. Without it a 3,000 s Isp thruster wins the mass cascade on an impulsive budget it cannot fly.
- **Extraction is rate-limited**: payload capped by what the rig can dig inside `max_mining_duration_yr`; dig time feeds mission duration, ops cost and WACC. Removing that cap makes the rig an infinitely fast vacuum.
- **M-type is not a bare metal core.** No M-type has ever been measured near iron-meteorite density (Psyche ≈ 3.8–3.9 g/cm³); metal fractions set accordingly — don't restore the 0.80/5.30 values.
- **A default run producing zero viable missions is the correct answer, not a regression.** So does every other combination currently in the model.

## Power / tanks (the circular dependency)

- **The beneficiation power plant feeds back into the rocket equation**: processing energy (Module 3: 200 Wh/kg dug, 500 Wh/kg concentrated) over stay time → power draw; Module 3's 60 W/kg-at-1-AU row scaled by 1/r² at target semi-major axis → array mass; launched like everything else. Payload → feed → power → mass → payload is a genuine circular dependency solved by fixed-point iteration in `evaluate_combo`. The 1/r² term: a 3+ AU target needs ~54× heavier plant than 1 AU.
- **Past 3.46 AU the plant should not be solar at all** (v1.11.0): solar is 60 W/kg @1AU falling as 1/r²; RTG ≈ 5 W/kg flat; sqrt(60/5) = 3.46 AU where they cross. Module 3 priced RTGs since v1.2.0 and nothing read the row until then — distant bodies were punished for an architecture no real outer-system mission makes. Not a free win: a radioisotope watt costs **625×** a solar one ($500k vs $800); model buys smallest that does the job; `rtg_max_power_w` caps because binding constraint is Pu-238 supply (~1.5 kg/yr, about one flagship RTG a year for the entire world), not money. **Do NOT extend to the EP array** (hundreds of kW) — pricing it off a radioisotope row quietly invents nuclear-electric propulsion.
- **A propellant's tank is mass and scales with VOLUME** (v1.11.0): LH2 0.0708 kg/L vs kerolox 1.015 = fourteen times the tank per kg burnt; before v1.11.0 hydrolox got its 452 s with no volumetric penalty at all. `tank_kg_per_L` derived per storage class from flight articles (hydrolox lands at 9.7% of propellant mass vs Centaur's measured ~9.7%), enters the closed-form cascade through `k = 1/(1 − t(R_ret − 1))` and `k_out`. **t(R−1) ≥ 1 means the tank cannot close — infeasible, not merely expensive** (same condition Module 2 hits on δ·R ≥ 1).

## Correctness invariants that were expensive to find

- **Designation extraction must not use a naive `^\d+` regex**: for "2024 BX1" it yields "2024", cross-matching unrelated bodies. See `_extract_canonical_designation` (catalog.py).
- **Never build a merge key by stringifying a float column.** A numeric identifier pandas typed float64 renders as `"3.0"` — not null, not obviously wrong, joins nothing. Go through `Int64` first. This cost NEOWISE four releases of contributing zero rows. The shape generalises: **the dtype depends on the data** — a source returning only numbered bodies in a small test slice is int64 and works; one unnumbered row appears and it silently breaks at scale. Anything that tests clean at a small row cap but is only ever run large is a candidate.
- **`str.contains` needs `regex=False`** in every lookup helper: designations/mineral names carry metacharacters — "(1) Ceres" matched "1 Ceres"; unbalanced brackets raised re.PatternError.
- **TPS mass belongs INSIDE the rocket-equation cascade**, not just the cost model: hauled outbound as dead mass and pushed back through the return burn. Omitting it overstates max payload by ~30%.
- **The return-capsule volume cap must BIND, not merely be reported** — the only constraint keeping a mission physical when ISRU is on and aerocapture off.
- **Composition fractions sum to 0.76–0.96, not 1.0**; residual valued at a bulk-silicate floor rather than zero.
- Do NOT globally suppress warnings in catalog.py — real RuntimeWarnings (divide-by-zero in derived physical columns) must stay visible.
- **Never `.astype(bool)` on a flag that arrives through a CSV** (or any column a future row could leave blank): it reads the string "False" as True AND NaN as True. Happens to be correct today only because every propellant row states `restartable` and `propellantless`; add one omitting either and a solid motor silently rejoins the search, no error anywhere. Use `_truthy(series, default=...)`. Second instance sat in transportation.py for eight releases: NaN half of the trap — a row omitting the flag got classed as a sail and dropped from BOTH Isp band and price band at once (checks stopped covering exactly the row most likely to be new). Fixed with `.ne(True)` resolved into `has_mass_ratio`; deliberately NOT `.fillna(False).astype(bool)`, which raises pandas FutureWarning on an object column — i.e. exactly when it would fire.
- **The three definitions that cost someone an hour each** (verify.py is the executable statement; all committed figures use these):

| quantity | it IS | it is NOT |
|---|---|---|
| objective ranked on | `total_cost_usd / gross_value_usd` | any column — **there is no `cost_revenue_ratio`** |
| `r` in every never-worse table | the LOWER setting's ratio over the HIGHER's (e.g. benef/raw) | the reciprocal |
| "median improvement +42.5%" | `median(1 − r)` — fractional REDUCTION in the ratio | `median(1/r − 1)`, which reads **74.0%** for the same result (factor of 1.7 apart; a harness picking wrong reports a number that is not wrong so much as *not the one on record*) |

- A fourth, and it's a DENOMINATOR: "bodies declining to concentrate" has two right answers — over JOINED raw+beneficiated pairs it's 15.8%; over the beneficiated run's own `concentration_ratio <= 1.0` column it's **15.61%** (bodies evaluable beneficiated but not raw are in one denominator and not the other). Both correct — quote which one.
- **Never-worse invariant** (one-liner over two archived runs): join raw + beneficiated catalogs for a destination on `designation`, assert `benef_ratio <= raw_ratio` row by row. Across all five destinations, 165,843 pairs: zero exceptions, worst case exactly 1.0000 — the signature of beneficiation declining and falling back to its baseline. **A max above 1.0 means the search is optimising something other than what gets reported.**

## Corrections (model terms that default ON)

Every model listed under README "What the model charges for" defaults ON, and each moved every number when it landed — they are corrections, not options; the flags exist to isolate an effect, not to be left off. The test for membership is NOT "is it on by default": **was the model getting something for free before?** (Neither `use_beneficiation` nor `optimise_programme_scale` passes that test — they are search axes, and calc 1.17.0 flipped both to True.)
